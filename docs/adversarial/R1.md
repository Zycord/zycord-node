# Zycord — Adversarial Review R1

**Scope:** whitepaper v0.1 + architecture spec v0.1. Personas: malicious miner, economic attacker, literal-minded implementer. Every finding lists attack → impact → fix. Fixes marked ✅ are applied in architecture v0.2 and the whitepaper erratum; ⚠ are accepted-and-documented residual risks.

---

## CRITICAL

### C1 — Re-billing griefing: one signature, many bills ✅
**Attack.** Spec v0.1 billed `EXPIRED` and `REPLAY` outcomes to the deposit, and did **not** mark billed-skipped certificates as seen. A malicious miner can therefore (a) withhold a certificate until its TTL passes, then include it expired, burning the victim's skip fee; (b) re-include an already-applied certificate — its refunded deposit cell still has balance, so the reserve succeeds and the victim is billed again; (c) re-include a stale-skipped certificate in every subsequent block within the TTL window, billing the same signature dozens of times. The skip fee being burned (not paid to the miner) does not stop this: it is pure griefing/extortion at near-zero attacker cost, against every user of the chain.
**Impact.** Total: deposits of arbitrary victims drainable by any block producer; with Phase-1 co-signers the same attack drains sequencer **bonds** — an existential economic hole.
**Fix (spec v0.2, whitepaper erratum).** Two-part invariant, now stated as the system's billing law — **"one signature, at most one bill, never at a position its signer could not avoid"**: (1) every *billed* outcome (`SKIPPED_*`) marks the certificate id seen, exactly like `APPLIED`; (2) including a certificate that is expired (`TTL < height` — stateless check) or already seen (stateful, but every full node holds the seen set) makes the **block invalid**. A signer accepts staleness risk by signing; it never accepts miner-manufactured expiry or duplication. `DROPPED` remains unbilled and unseen (re-submittable once funded).
**Where the law lives now.** It is unchanged and it is normative text rather than this review's to state: [ARCHITECTURE §10](../ARCHITECTURE.md) carries *one signature, at most one bill, never at a position its signer could not avoid* as the audit target, with the mechanics (billed skips mark seen at F6/F7; B1–B4 make seen, expired and under-bid certificates unincludable) and the griefing suite that exists to break it.

### C2 — No intra-underwriter ordering in Era 0 ✅
**Attack/bug.** v0.1 ordered the fold by `(first-sig pubkey, cert_id)`. `cert_id` is a hash — effectively random. Two dependent certificates from the same user in one block (receive-then-spend, spend-then-sweep — the *default* one-shot wallet pattern) commit in random order; the dependent one skips `STALE` and, under C1's old rules, is billed for a self-conflict it never created. The whitepaper's data structure had the `seq` field; the architecture spec dropped it for Era 0.
**Fix.** `Seq uint64` restored to the certificate (signed, user-chosen). Canonical fold order: `(underwriter_id, seq, cert_id)` where Era-0 `underwriter_id` = deposit-cell owner address. Wallets increment `seq` per dependent chain. Phase-1 sequencer pipelines inherit the identical rule.

### C3 — One-shot `spent` semantics: three interacting bugs ✅
**Attack/bug (i) — refund into a burned cell.** The implicit MARK_SPENT on first debit fires before settlement; the deposit refund then targets a spent cell and either fails or strands funds.
**(ii) — billing vs. guards.** Reserve/refund/fee credits were ordinary writes in v0.1, subject to spent/guard checks — under-specified and circular.
**(iii) — address resurrection.** Pruning spent one-shot cells *deleted the spent bit*: the address reads as fresh (∅), can be re-credited (payments to a dead address silently burn) and even **reused**, breaking the one-shot invariant that wallets and privacy assumptions rely on.
**Fix.** (i) `MARK_SPENT` becomes an **explicit write**, required by V-rule in any certificate that debits a `0x01` address, applied at F8 — settlement (F9) is ordered after it but is exempt (see ii). Deposits gain a `RefundTo` slot so refunds never target the burned cell. (ii) Billing operations (reserve, settle, refund, fee/emission credits) are **fold-level primitives**, exempt from spent checks and guards except the reserve's own `GUARD_GE` — the same exemption coinbase always needed, now uniform. (iii) Pruning may delete cell *values*, never spent *bits*: the spent-address registry is permanent consensus state (see L4).

## HIGH

### H1 — Market-priced skip fee makes deposits unboundable ✅
**Attack.** If `skip_fee = k × base_fee`, a certificate signed at base fee X can be committed up to TTL later; base fee moves ±12.5%/block, so the worst-case multiplier over a 240-block TTL is astronomically larger than X. Either deposits must over-reserve absurdly or the ceiling check (V5) is unsound.
**Fix.** `SKIP_FEE` is a **protocol constant** (genesis parameter, changeable only by fork). Skip pricing is anti-spam, not fine resource pricing; constancy is what makes the deposit ceiling statelessly checkable.
**Where it lives now.** It is still a constant in `spec/params.json`, and [ARCHITECTURE §10](../ARCHITECTURE.md) is what states it — together with the V5 ceiling the constancy buys, which R2-H1 later rewrote around the two-field bid without touching this argument.

### H2 — Wall-clock block validity forks the network ✅
**Attack.** "`Header.Time < local + 90 s`" makes block *validity* depend on each node's clock: at the boundary, half the network accepts and half rejects the same block — a view fork an attacker can manufacture at will by timestamping at the edge.
**Fix.** Bitcoin's rule: future-dated blocks (beyond FTL = 180 s) are **withheld, not invalid** — queued and re-evaluated as the local clock advances. Only the median-past bound is a validity rule. LWMA clamps absorb the rest.
**Where it lives now**, and it is the finding in this review that has grown the most since. [ARCHITECTURE §12](../ARCHITECTURE.md) owns the time rules, the withhold queue and what the median bound permits in the backward direction; [spec/wire.md §9](../spec/wire.md) rule 8 states the withhold normatively, including the ordering that stops a header carrying no work from being laundered into "nothing to do this pass". The last sentence above is the one to read carefully: the LWMA clamps did **not** absorb the rest — a signed, symmetrically clamped solve-time accumulator, a retarget normalised against the window's *average* target rather than its last one, and the future-time limit itself are what that took.

### H3 — Fee bid below base fee is undefined at commit ✅
**Attack.** Base fee rises after signing; an included certificate cannot pay its bid. Billing the gap to the deposit punishes the signer for market motion (C1's principle violated); silently dropping invites stuffing.
**Fix.** Block-validity rule: every included certificate satisfies `bid ≥ block base fee` (deterministic, checkable by all). Honest builders filter; the dry-run they already perform makes this free.

### H4 — Unbounded TTL = immortal seen-set entries ✅
**Attack.** v0.1 bounded TTL only in mempool policy. A miner self-includes certificates with `TTL = 2^64`; their seen entries never prune — permanent state growth at ~40 B/cert, forever, for pennies.
**Fix.** Consensus V-rule: `TTL − inclusion-height ≤ TTL_MAX (240)` — enforced at block validity, not just relay.

## MEDIUM

### M1 — Multi-move TRANSFER guards check pre-state independently ✅
Two moves debiting the same source cell each pass `GUARD_GE` against the *pre-certificate* balance; correctness currently falls out of the F7 overflow catch — an accident, not a rule. **Fix:** V3 derivation **merges per-slot debits** into one guard + one delta before comparison. (General lesson recorded: guards within one certificate must be evaluated against one coherent snapshot; derivation must canonicalize.)

### M2 — MINT operand underflow ✅
`GUARD_LE(minted, cap − Δ)` with `Δ > cap` underflows in uint space. **Fix:** V3 derivation rejects `Δ > cap` (both values are in the certificate — stateless).

### M3 — Body-fetch DoS ordering ✅
Hash-first relay must verify **header PoW before fetching bodies**; otherwise ghost announcements are free. Now explicit, with peer-scoring on unserved bodies.

Still the rule, and normative rather than this review's to state: [spec/wire.md §9](../spec/wire.md) rules 3–5 require bodies to be requested only along a header chain already validated and already winning on accumulated work, and make an advertised body the announcer owes — charged once when the wait window elapses. §10.1 generalises the ordering into the ingress cost rule every handler is measured against.

### M4 — Genesis ceilings vs 30-s propagation ✅
20 k certs/block (~10 MB) at launch hashrate and peer counts risks fork-rate blowup exactly when the chain is weakest. **Fix:** genesis ceilings start conservative (4 k certs, tighter gas/byte caps); raising them later is a soft, forkless parameter change only in the upward-compatible sense documented in `spec/params.json` governance notes.

The conservative-at-genesis half is live; the *raising* half has since been given a rule of its own, and the qualification above turned out to be the whole difficulty. [decisions/capacity-eras.md](../decisions/capacity-eras.md) classifies each structural capacity by what re-pinning it costs — `CertListCapacity` is an encoding fork and is therefore sized at genesis for the whole curve, the gas and byte capacities are params-only and move at era boundaries — and it cites this finding for why a young network's fork rate is the binding constraint.

## LOW / NOTES

- **L1 — Beacon entropy is miner-grindable** (it is a PoW block id). Documented: unfit for high-value lotteries until Phase-2 VRF. ⚠
- **L2 — Reproducible builds with cgo** require pinning the C toolchain and RandomX commit in the build container, not just Go. Added to P6. ✅
- **L3 — First ~100 blocks are miner-only** (coinbase maturity + mandatory deposits ⇒ no funded depositor exists yet). Not a bug — "mine to play" is the correct memecoin genesis dynamic. Documented. ✅
- **L4 — Permanent spent-address registry** (from C3-iii): ~33 B per one-shot address, forever — the same structural cost as Monero's nullifier set. At payments scale this becomes the dominant state item; acceptable for Era 0, flagged as the protocol's honest open problem with accumulator-based compaction as the research direction. ⚠
- **L5 — Whitepaper/spec drift** (expired-billing pseudocode, missing `seq`, "TRANSFer" typo) reconciled; the architecture doc §0 rule ("this document wins") worked as intended. ✅

## POSITIVE FINDING

### N1 — The Era-0 op set is poisoning-immune (should be a stated theorem)
The classic attack on declared-input systems is **stale-griefing**: credit 1 unit to a cell someone EXACT-read and their certificate skips at their expense. Auditing the native ops shows the attack has **no surface in Era 0**: TRANSFER uses only `GUARD_GE` on balances (third-party *credits* cannot falsify a ≥-guard; debits need the owner's own signature), MINT's EXACT reads target immutable cells (can never go stale), ISSUE writes only to cells derived from its own cert id, RETIRE touches only owner cells. Consequence: **every billed skip in Era 0 is either self-inflicted (own double-spend) or a genuine supply-cap race** — no third party can cause another user's certificate to skip. This is a real theorem about the op set, now stated in §9 and pinned by a dedicated adversarial test suite ("griefing suite", §18). EXACT-read poisoning returns as a concern only with the cEVM in Phase 1 — exactly where professional co-signers exist to price and absorb it.

---

**Disposition:** architecture spec rewritten as v0.2 with all ✅ fixes integrated; whitepaper patched with the single-billing erratum (fold pseudocode + replay note). Residual risks: L1, L4 — both documented in-spec as open problems rather than hidden.
