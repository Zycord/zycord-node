# Zycord — Adversarial Review R2

**Scope:** a full second reading of [I1](I1.md) and [H2-economics](H2-economics.md) against architecture spec v0.3 — the review of the review, and of the M0 implementation those documents describe.

**Persona:** the reader who assumes the previous pass stopped where it felt finished. Two questions throughout: *where did a choice get made without being weighed?* and *where does "sorted", "usage" or "unreachable" mean two things?*

**Verdict.** I1 ratified in full. H2-economics ratified on the miner side and **reopened on the user side**, where a decision had been taken without a rejected-alternatives section — which turned out to be exactly where the finding was.

---

## The process finding, stated first

I1 carried a confidence section listing the choices its author judged unforced. Every item on that list was somewhere a second reader was genuinely needed. The one significant gap in this batch was **not** on that list, and could not have been: it was in the fee structure, which felt forced and therefore went unexamined.

**Rule adopted.** An economic mechanism gets a rejected-alternatives section *even when the choice feels forced.* "Feels forced" is the feeling of not having looked. In consensus code the compiler and the differential fold notice an unexamined choice; in the economic layer nothing does, because every internally-consistent fee rule compiles and every one of them passes a differential against itself.

## HIGH

### R2-H1 — The bid structure was first-price, and the alternative was never weighed ✅ *fixed; certificate format changed*

**Problem.** H2-economics decided who receives the money and left unexamined what the signer offers. It adopted pay-your-bid — `charge = gas × bid` — without recording the two-field `(max, priority)` structure that EIP-1559 exists to provide. The most-studied decision in the history of fee markets was absent from a document whose subject was fee-market decisions.

**Why it matters here rather than in general.** This is not the classical bid-shading argument against first-price auctions; a public base fee genuinely mitigates that, and it is why pay-your-bid looked defensible. It is an interaction between three of Zycord's own rules:

- B4 makes a certificate unincludable once the base fee passes its price;
- `TTL_MAX` lets a certificate wait 240 blocks for inclusion;
- the base fee moves ±12.5% per block.

A signer who wants to survive that window must name a price far above today's base fee. Under pay-your-bid that safety margin is **charged in full whenever the certificate is included early** — a structural overpayment equal to the signer's own risk margin, on every certificate, forever, and larger the more cautiously they play.

**Fix.** `FeeBid` becomes four fields: a maximum and a priority per market. Per market, `effective = min(priority, max − base)`, `burn = gas × base`, `tip = gas × effective`. The maximum is a solvency bound (B4 becomes `max ≥ base`) and is never charged; the priority is what a miner is actually paid. Canonical form requires `priority ≤ max`.

**Accepted cost, recorded rather than glossed.** The deposit reserves against `gas × max`, so a large buffer is free in *fees* but not in *locked balance* — and the protection is therefore weakest for the smallest balances, who can least afford to name a large maximum. The lockup is intra-block (reserved at F3, refunded at F9), and the alternative charges *everyone* their margin rather than merely bounding how large a margin the poorest can name. Wallets must size headroom against available balance; recorded in [WALLET.md](../WALLET.md).

**Rejected alternatives** are now in H2-economics §7.5: pay-your-bid; relaxing B4 (reintroduces R1-H3 exactly); shortening `TTL_MAX` to shrink the buffer (bending a latency parameter to paper over a fee bug); priority as a fraction of base (re-couples ordering to market motion).

Pinned by `TestSafetyBufferIsFree` — raising the maximum with priority and base fixed must not change the charge — and `TestEarlyInclusionNeverCostsMore`.

### R2-H2 — "Usage" was never defined, and the wrong reading is a cheap market lever ✅ *spec gap*

**Problem.** I1-H1 had the base fees update "from its own gas usage after the certificates have run", without saying whose gas. If skipped certificates count, an attacker stuffs blocks with self-conflicting certificates — self-inflicted, so the billing law is untouched and the theorem holds — at the constant `SKIP_FEE` each, and drives the base fee up for everybody. Because `SKIP_FEE` is constant by design (R1-H1), the attack's cost does not rise with the damage it does. It is griefing rather than theft, since base fees burn, but it prices the whole network from a lever that costs a constant.

**Fix.** Three quantities, defined separately in §15 and implemented separately:

| Quantity | Counts | Drives |
|---|---|---|
| Included gas | every certificate in the block, any outcome | the per-block gas ceilings — they bound physical work |
| **Applied gas** | the certificates that **APPLIED** | the base-fee update at F12b, and nothing else |
| Block bytes | every included certificate, drops included | the byte ceiling |

A skip is a failed attempt to use the chain, not evidence of demand for it.

Pinned by `TestSkipsAreNotDemand` and the `skips-do-not-move-the-base-fee` vector. The test tightens the gas ceiling on purpose: at devnet's ceiling a handful of certificates is a rounding error against the target and the base fee lands on the same value either way — the assertion would pass without asserting anything. It now proves that counting the skipped gas *would* have given a different answer, so it cannot pass by coincidence.

The three-way split is live, and normative text owns it rather than this review: [ARCHITECTURE §15](../ARCHITECTURE.md) carries it as *three gas quantities, three jobs*, next to the F12b input clamp that came later — a block may carry up to `4T` under the burst valve, so F12b's sequential input is clamped at `2T` to keep the base-fee step symmetric.

## MEDIUM

### R2-M1 — Saturating arithmetic where an assertion belongs ✅ *supersedes I1-L9*

I1-L9 left fold-level credits saturating, on the grounds that the alternative — a panic — converts an impossible arithmetic condition into a liveness failure. The objection was right and the instrument was wrong: `ApplyBlock` already reports block-invalidity, so an impossible condition should return exactly that. A block that trips it is rejected and the node keeps serving other branches; if every block trips it, the node halts loudly, which is the correct failure mode for money and strictly better than destroying value silently and finding out in an audit.

There is also a self-referential reason, worth recording: **the I1-L8 unreachability proof rests on conservation, and saturation is precisely a conservation-violating operation.** Had it ever executed, it would have eroded the invariant that proves it cannot. The assertion protects the premise of the proof of its own uselessness.

### R2-M2 — "Sorted" admits equal neighbours; strictly increasing does not ✅ *canonical-form hole*

I1-M4's minimality closure and the seen set's one-transition-one-form property both silently assumed no duplicates. Strict ordering is now enforced **at decode**, as a canonical-form rule rather than a later check, for reads, writes, signatures and retire lists — with negative tests for each.

**Noted, not fixed:** `TRANSFER` moves have no canonical order, so two orderings of the same moves are two certificates with two ids and the same effect. That is not the same property. Two different certificates having the same effect is unavoidable in any format that lets a signer split an amount, and forbidding it would be arbitrary; and reordering the moves changes the body, so only a signer can produce the variant, which is indistinguishable from that signer authorising a second payment. Transition-level canonicality is neither achievable nor needed.

**Superseded, and it is worth saying how.** This entry used to justify itself with the sentence *"One certificate has one encoding, which is what the seen set requires."* That sentence was false, and a certificate-id malleability finding is what it cost. One certificate had unboundedly many encodings — the signature list is chosen by the signers, not by the format, and every Ed25519 nonce yields another valid one. The seen set does not require one encoding per certificate and never could; it requires one **key** per authorization, which is what the id now is.

What the id *is* is normative rather than this review's to define, and [spec/wire.md §3.1](../spec/wire.md) is what owns it: the certificate id names an authorization and MUST NOT cover the signature list, the exemplar hash names one encoding and is what `CertRoot` is a root over, and the two obligations that follow — a block commits to the signature bytes it carries, and relay treats exemplars rather than ids — are stated there with the equivocation each one closes.

### R2-S — Confirmations ✅

1. **Maturity-ring bootstrap** — the release step is a defined no-op while an entry is empty: no zero-address credit, no phantom cell. Vector `maturity-ring-bootstrap` at height 1.
2. **The ring matures all tips** — I1-C2's pre-H2 wording said "parallel tips"; corrected to all tips, and the implementation routes both markets' tips through the same ring. A miner paid instantly while emission matured would reintroduce the reorg asymmetry the ring exists to remove.
3. **Parameter invariants** — generalised from I1-L11 into one blanket rule: *every numeric parameter is positive, no exceptions.* Enforced by walking the struct, so a parameter added later is covered without anybody remembering to cover it, and swept by a reflective test that fails if it ever checks fewer than the fields present. `TTL_MAX ≥ 2` and gas ceilings `≥ 2` (their targets are divisors) added alongside.
4. **I1-M6 and I1-L3 into normative text** — the beacon-entropy choice is now recorded in §6 *with its rationale*, and the `min_base_fee` trap is a note carried inside `params.json` itself.

## Ratifications worth recording

- **I1-M3 is the find of the batch.** Undo carrying seen-set *removals* closes a reorg-shaped reopening of R1-C1 that neither adversarial pass saw, because neither held prune (F10) and undo (F15) in mind at once. This is the class of bug only implementation surfaces.
- **I1-M6 ratified as a decision, not an inheritance.** `ParentID` is not merely the available reading, it is the better one: a block a whole epoch back is known an epoch in advance, maximising the prediction window; `ParentID` is revealed only at the boundary. Both remain grindable and R1-L1 stands verbatim.
- **I1-H3's correction of the reviewer stands.** The bound on the carve-out is *being named* by the certificate, not sharing a signer with it. The amplification is a formula rather than a constant: `MAX_RETIRE_ADDRS × concurrent payers per address`, where the second factor is ~1 under the wallet rule and unbounded without it — which is what makes that rule a deliverable rather than folklore. Case 3 also has a good-faith variant with no malice at all: a payee *sweeps* a one-shot address, which marks it spent, racing a second payment to it. Same case, same rule: an address, once paid, is dead for payers too.

## Disposition

All findings implemented, mutation-checked, and covered by vectors or property tests. The certificate format changed under R2-H1, so the whole golden corpus moved — which is what a consensus change is supposed to look like in a diff.

Two documents follow from this review rather than from any single finding: [WALLET.md](../WALLET.md), which consolidates the behavioural contract the findings had quietly accumulated, and the release-gate section of [RELEASE.md](../RELEASE.md), which fixes the publication moment at M3.

**No layer is final.** This review found what I1 missed; the implementation of this review's own R2-H1 then found a deposit regression that this review missed when specifying it (see [H2-economics §7.4](H2-economics.md)). Neither is an embarrassment and both are the point: the credibility of the process is the stack, not any one stratum, and a document that claimed otherwise would be the least trustworthy thing in the repository.

**Next:** M1, then [I2](I2.md) in the same format — confidence section included. It has found things three times now.
